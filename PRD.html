Executive Strategy & PRD

Greenroom 2.0: The Executable Ledger

Transitioning from a passive CMS to a relationship intelligence and financial operating system for live music.

Author
Prithvi Rey
Date
May 2026
Focus Area
Settlement, Intelligence & Provenance
One-Line Framing: We are replacing a brittle, read-only calculator with an interactive, PR-style settlement workspace—enforcing caps natively, establishing receipt provenance, and tracking deal versions to eliminate 2 a.m. signature chaos.

1. Why This Work Matters

Greenroom is an $8.1M ARR SaaS winning on platform completeness, but fundamentally failing at the most critical trust junction: financial settlement.

When money moves, clarity is the only feature that matters. Currently, only 18% of our customers use our settlement tool. Our database forensics revealed why: the system's hardcoded logic fails on 63% of deals (Vs Deals, % of Net). More dangerously, the system silently leaks margin—our data showed 222 shows (44%) settled with expenses exceeding their contractual cap, resulting in $175,664 of unchecked overages absorbed by venues.

If left unaddressed, this product degrades from a system of record into a liability. Users are retreating to Google Sheets for math and email for disputes. This feature slice reclaims the financial payload, protects venue margins, and restores institutional trust.

Metric Value What It Means
Tool adoption rate 18% 82% of users settle outside the product (Google Sheets).
Deals tool cannot settle 63% Vs deals, % of net, door deals are fundamentally broken.
Expense cap breaches 44% 222 shows with overages—none flagged systematically.
Total silent overage $175,664 Margin passed through to artists without detection.
Disputed recoup value $8,083 Frozen payments from unresolved agent disputes.
TM Sign-off method 👍 Signatures are currently collected via emoji in a freetext field.

2. Problem Space & Product Understanding

Live venue settlement is not a clean, synchronous accounting event. It is a messy, relational negotiation executed by exhausted people in a dark back office.

The current architecture treats settlement as a static database read operation. But for Mariana (the Lead Booker), settlement is a high-stakes write operation. She is navigating ambiguous 80-word email agreements, tracking undocumented expenses, and defending her venue's margin.

The Ghost Deal

Deals are amended via phone calls days before a show. Bookers note this in free-text, but structured DB fields remain stale. The UI actively lies about the math.

The Dual-Audience

The Tour Manager signs off on physical cash at 2 a.m., but the Agent audits contract caps on Monday morning. One monolithic "Disputed" status cannot model this reality.

I entered this domain looking at standard SaaS metrics, but quickly realized settlement is not a standard SaaS workflow. It is a high-stakes, adversarial negotiation executed by exhausted people.

The "Vs Deal" Complexity

63% of deals are "Vs deals" (e.g., "$3500 vs 85% of net"). Current DB models use rigid `guarantee` and `percentage` columns, failing entirely when dynamic caps or sliding scales are introduced via free-text deal notes.

The Zero Provenance Trap

The UI operates on trust, not proof. A $300 hospitality line item is just a number. When disputed by an agent on Monday, the venue has no embedded cryptographic or receipt-based proof, leading to margin erosion.

3. My Process & Discovery

I did not start by drawing screens. I started by tearing down the data architecture to trace the symptom (spreadsheet abandonment) to its root cause.

  1. Ethnographic Synthesis: I mapped the differing incentives. Mariana needs margin defense. Diego (TM) needs verification and speed. Sarah (Agent) needs provenance and audit trails.
  2. Database Forensics (SQLite): I executed deep queries on the starter repo data. I didn't just ask what was breaking; I asked where the money was leaking. This led to the discovery of the "Door Deal Bloodbath" (average -$983 loss for venues).
  3. State Machine Analysis: I discovered negative timestamps causing JS runtime crashes. This proved our status column suffered from race conditions (shows being signed and disputed simultaneously).
  4. FigJam Hypothesis Mapping: I mapped the current flow (rigid DB → failed math → Sheet fallback → Email dispute) against an ideal operational flow (Pre-flight Triage → PR-style Review → Immutable Sign-off).
Crucial Discovery: The data revealed that Hospitality and Marketing were the most disputed categories ($12k+ tied up). Building a better calculator wasn't enough; we had to build an evidence layer (mandatory receipt uploads).

4. The Core Insight

The turning point in the product strategy came from a single realization:

We were forcing a dynamic financial negotiation into a static CMS.

Mariana wasn't using spreadsheets because they were prettier. She used them because a spreadsheet shows its work. It is legible, defensible, and non-destructive.

Greenroom's UI was a "Black Box" that spit out a final total_to_artist number. The solution wasn't generic dashboards. The solution was an Executable Ledger—an exact-input interface that explicitly writes out the conditional logic of a contract on the screen, accompanied by an intelligent triage system to prevent disputes before they happen.

6. Product Narrative

The user journey shifts from reactive data-entry to proactive relationship operations.

  • 1. Intelligence (/artists): Before booking, Mariana checks the Artist Health Score and Agent Dispute Risk to structure a safe deal.
  • 2. Triage (/shows): On Wednesday, Mariana reviews the "Needs Attention" column, catching unverified expense caps before the show.
  • 3. Preparation (/shows/[id]): Show day. Expenses are entered with receipt proofs. Phone amendments are logged cleanly in the Deal Version history.
  • 4. Execution (/shows/[id]/settle): 2:00 AM. Mariana and Diego review the PR-style calculation. They comment, adjust, and confirm the payout with total mathematical confidence.

7. PRD: Final Product Direction

Based directly on the final agentic development prompt, here is the functional direction for the core routes:

A. The Relationship Intelligence Layer (/artists)

Intent: Transform a flat directory into an operational CRM.

  • Controls: Sort by Health Score, Revenue, Dispute Risk. Filter by Agency.
  • Artist Profile Cards: Compact modules detailing Booking History (deal mix, payout avg, expense compliance rate).
  • Agent Intelligence: Surface the "Agent Dispute Risk" (e.g., warning if dealing with a historically combative agent).
  • Action: "Book Again" shortcut pre-filling deal terms based on past success.

B. The Triage Dashboard (/shows)

Intent: Shift from a chron-list to an operations command surface.

  • Top Dashboard Strip: Low-noise metrics (Unsettled count, disputed count, total pending payouts).
  • Three-Column Triage: Group shows into This Weekend, Needs Attention (e.g., overdue signatures), and Unresolved (stale disputes).

C. The Deal Workspace & Calculator Tile (/shows/[id])

Intent: Establish the source of truth for deal versioning and gross logic.

  • Deal Versioning: An immutable timeline showing what changed, when, and who changed it (e.g., "Phone call: Reduced guarantee to $6000").
  • Expense Proofs: Expenses require a description and receipt upload link.
  • Gross Math Panel: Explicitly display "Fees In" vs "Fees Out" to resolve basis ambiguity.
  • The Bauhaus Calculator Tile: In the right-side empty space, an elegant, Teenage Engineering-inspired working calculator. Users can modify assumptions locally, test mathematical logic like Excel, and push the confirmed result to the settlement route.

D. The Collaborative Settlement (/shows/[id]/settle)

Intent: A PR-style review page to safely audit and execute the payout.

  • Invoice / Math Waterfall: A cascading vertical breakdown showing exactly how the "Total to Artist" was derived (Gross → Deductions → Winning Share → Bonus).
  • Collaboration Model: Agents/TMs can leave comments on specific sections, flag disputes, or request changes without breaking the underlying DB data.
  • Confirmation Flow: Clear reviewer name input and "Confirm Payout" action.

8. Design & HCI Rationale

The visual language treats the software as a high-end operational instrument, not a generic SaaS template.

  • The Bauhaus Calculator: Added to the show page right-panel. By designing this like precision hardware (stark contrasts, clear input affordances, zero decorative fluff), we induce a psychological feeling of accuracy. It serves as a safe "sandbox" to test deal math.
  • Tabular Numerals & Monospace: All financial data utilizes monospace fonts. This ensures decimal alignment, speeds up visual scanning at 2 a.m., and communicates immutability.
  • Information Density: The UI groups related variables tightly (e.g., Expenses + Receipts). We avoided oversized hero metrics in favor of compact, scannable triage columns.
  • Explicit Traceability: Instead of hiding the math, the UI writes it out: MAX(2631, 90% * (Gross - Fees - Allowed Expenses)). This acts as an instant conversational defense for Mariana.

9. Trade-offs & What I Cut

To ship a high-confidence V1 that immediately stops margin leakage, severe scoping decisions were made:

Intentional Cuts

  • Full Spreadsheet Calculation Engine: We built a constrained Bauhaus calculator tile, but intentionally did not build a full Excel clone. Full spreadsheets invite unstructured data chaos. We accept the risk that ultra-complex 4-tier deals may still require external tools.
  • Deep Backend Permissions (RBAC): V1 focuses on UI-level PR-style reviews. A full backend granular permissions system (TM vs Agent vs Booker edit rights) was cut to speed up delivery.
  • Automated Payment Rails: While the "Weekend Liquidity Gap" is real, integrating Stripe Treasury for automated payouts was scoped out. V1 handles the math consensus; the physical wire transfer remains a manual operational step.

10. Validation Plan

I would validate this slice through strict operational and behavioral instrumentation:

  • Operational Metric: Track the dollar amount of "Venue Absorbed Losses" captured by the new cap compliance warnings. Target: Zero silent cap breaches in 30 days.
  • Behavioral Metric: Measure adoption of the /shows/[id]/settle tool for Vs Deals. Target: Increase in-app settlement from 18% → 60%+.
  • Qualitative Usability: Shadow Mariana during a Friday midnight settlement. Observe the Time-to-Sign-off. Target: Reduce negotiation time from 45 mins → 10 mins using the new PR-style view.
  • Dispute Reduction: Track post-settlement comments/disputes. Target: 50% drop in agent-initiated Monday emails due to upfront receipt provenance.

11. What I'd Ship Next

The roadmap sequences from core utility to ecosystem automation.

  1. Immediate (Q2): Partial Resolution Workflow. Allow undisputed funds (base guarantee) to be confirmed and wired immediately, isolating only the disputed delta (e.g., a $400 hospitality charge) in a separate resolution queue.
  2. Medium-Term (Q3): Advance Data Intake Portal. Shift data-entry labor by sending a secure web-form to Tour Managers weeks prior, auto-populating backline and hospitality requests into Greenroom's DB.
  3. Long-Term (Q4): The FinTech Clearing Hub. Solve the weekend cashflow gap by allowing the venue to trigger an instant payout to a TM debit card via embedded finance rails.

12. Technical, Data & Systems Depth

This implementation requires shifting from a mutable CMS to an immutable FinTech data architecture.

Data Modeling Adjustments:
  • deal_versions table: Required for the timeline UI. (id, show_id, changed_at, changed_by, old_payload, new_payload, note).
  • expenses update: Add receipt_url (string) and constrain description to be NOT NULL if amount > 100.
  • settlement_reviews table: To support the PR-style collaboration flow without overwriting the base settlement status. Tracks comments, approvals, and requested changes.

System Assumptions: The frontend relies heavily on client-side state for the "Bauhaus Calculator" to ensure zero-latency re-renders while testing deal math. We assume existing backend file-upload infrastructure can be repurposed for the receipt_url attachments.

13. Comparative Thinking

Generic SaaS CRM dashboards (like Salesforce) fail here because they treat a booking as a "won deal." In live music, booking the show is 10% of the work; executing the settlement is 90%.

Competitors like Prism.fm offer robust event lifecycle tracking, but often feel like heavy ERPs. They digitize the process but still largely rely on traditional form views. By framing the settlement page as a Pull Request Review (inspired by GitHub) combined with a Cryptographic Ledger (inspired by Carta), we differentiate Greenroom. We aren't just logging numbers; we are enforcing financial guardrails and managing multi-party trust programmatically.
